Many of my field jobs follow the same pattern: an engineering team runs the change from a bridge call, and I'm the person on site plugging things in, typing commands and reporting back. Here's the checklist I use.

## The day before

- **Read the work instructions end to end.** Not on site — the night before.
- **Build the USB drive.** Download and *extract* every package, then confirm the key files are there.
- **Print** the instructions and any validation form.
- **Charge everything:** laptop, phone, earbuds, a power bank.
- **Install the meeting app** on your phone and test audio.

## What goes in the bag

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| Laptop with admin rights | Running tools and checking connectivity |
| USB drive with extracted files | No downloads on hotel/guest Wi-Fi |
| Spare Cat 6 patch cable | The existing one is always too short or damaged |
| Network toner / tester | Finding the right port in an unlabeled closet |
| Small toolkit (incl. hex keys) | Mounting brackets and enclosures |
| Photo ID | Most sites check before letting you in |

## On site

1. **Arrive early** and check in with the on-site contact.
2. **Join the bridge on time** — use earbuds, never speakerphone in a shared space.
3. **Repeat every instruction back** before you do it ("Running step 4 on workstation 2 now").
4. **Take photos** of the rack, labels and finished install.
5. **Don't improvise** on production systems. If something differs from the guide, say so on the bridge and wait.

## Before you leave

- Confirm the remote team has validated everything.
- Complete the property validation form.
- Clean up packaging and leave the area tidy.
- Send a short closing note with photos.

Good field work is mostly preparation and clear communication — the technical part goes smoothly when those two are right.
